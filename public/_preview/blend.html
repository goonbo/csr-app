The morning we packed 1,200 boxes.

A two-hour shift in Bayview. Eighteen volunteers from the engineering and design orgs — half first-timers — paired with twelve regulars from the SF Marin team. Rain held off until lunch.

What happened

Volunteers split across three lines — produce, dry goods, and family-pack assembly. The dry goods station moved fastest after Maya from SF Marin showed the team a tighter folding pattern; that change alone added an estimated 180 boxes to the day's count.

Two new partner relationships opened up over the morning's coffee break. The food bank's volunteer coordinator suggested a recurring quarterly cadence — same crew, same Saturday — and three of the engineering team committed on the spot.

View · Insight
"Half-day Saturdays in Bayview consistently outperform weekday evening shifts on output per person. Maya's folding pattern is a repeatable training input — worth documenting before the next shift."

What we learned

First-timers felt useful inside the first ten minutes — a low ramp the regulars credited to the line setup. The exit survey came back with an average rating of 4.7 / 5 and a free-text request for "more like this, sooner."
